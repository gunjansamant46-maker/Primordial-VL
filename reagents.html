🧪 Reagents & Stains

Methylene Blue

Purpose: Used to stain animal cells, especially the nucleus, to make internal structures clearly visible.

Principle: A basic dye that binds to negatively charged DNA and RNA via electrostatic attraction.

Result: Nucleus appears deep blue, cytoplasm appears light blue.

Precaution: Avoid over-staining as it may obscure details.

Crystal Violet

Purpose: Primary stain used in Gram staining.

Principle: Forms a complex with iodine and is retained in Gram-positive bacteria.

Result: Gram-positive bacteria appear purple.

Precaution: Follow proper washing steps for accurate results.

Safranin

Purpose: Counterstain used in Gram staining.

Principle: Stains cells that do not retain crystal violet.

Result: Gram-negative bacteria appear pink/red.

Precaution: Avoid excessive staining.

Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)

Purpose: Used to acidify solutions and in hydrolysis reactions.

Principle: Releases hydrogen ions (H⁺), increasing acidity.

Result: Solution becomes highly acidic (low pH).

Precaution: Highly corrosive; handle with care.

Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH)

Purpose: Used to create basic solutions and neutralize acids.

Principle: Releases hydroxide ions (OH⁻), increasing alkalinity.

Result: Solution becomes strongly basic (high pH).

Precaution: Can cause burns; handle carefully.
